## Satire, parody and caricature

Politique 2027 is a work of humour. The characters are **caricatures of public figures** — French politicians, declared candidates (among them an athlete), a foreign head of state, the wife of the French president, a business founder, two video makers and a television journalist — depicted solely in connection with their public life. Their powers, titles and situations are made up for laughs: they are not news and not a statement of the author's opinions.

- **Photomontages.** Each public figure's head is a **real photograph**, published under a free licence on Wikimedia Commons, cropped and cut out, then placed on a drawn cardboard body. These are obviously **satirical montages**, as this notice expressly states. When a character changes expression (smiling, angry, struggling), it is another real photograph, chosen for its expression: no face is retouched, distorted or generated by artificial intelligence. The author and licence of every photo are listed below.
- **Catchphrases.** Some speech bubbles are public lines that became famous in France ("De la poudre de perlimpinpin", "Je traverse la rue, je vous trouve du travail", "La République, c'est moi !", "Moi président"…), quoted for what they are: cult lines. The other lines of the politicians are made up in the spirit of the character. None is offered as a documentary quotation.
- **Facts referred to.** Some powers nod to public facts: Marine Le Pen's "appeal to the Court of Cassation" refers to the Paris Court of Appeal's ruling of 7 July 2026, which she announced she would appeal (that court has not yet ruled); Jean-Luc Mélenchon's hologram to his 2017 rallies; "I cross the street" and the dissolution to words and decisions of Emmanuel Macron (2018, 2024); Éric Ciotti's "locked headquarters" to the Républicains headquarters that stayed shut in June 2024; Sandrine Rousseau's "no more barbecue" to her 2022 remark about barbecues; the "Frexit" to Florian Philippot's platform; Gérald Darmanin's handcuffs to his offices as a minister (Interior, then Justice). The game attributes no other fact to them.
- **Guests.** The two video makers, the journalist and the business founder appear as guests, and no statement is attributed to them beyond harmless lines or their own public posts. One of the video makers and the journalist question every camp in the same way; the journalist's power ("follow the money") refers to the genre of the TV investigation, not to any particular report. The other video maker publicly took a stand against the far right in 2024, so he only appears against the Right camp: this reflects that public stance, not a bias of the game. The business founder appears in reference to a public exchange of November 2024 (a "1v1" a gamer had offered him outside a supermarket in Marseille, to which he replied publicly); his only line is his own post. His power ("network down") is a joke on that meme: it neither describes nor judges his company's service.
- **Institutions and services.** The Constitutional Council appears as an anonymous "sage": none of its members is depicted. The teachers, paramedics, baker, riot police, firefighters and speed camera are generic figures.
- **Media.** A rolling-news channel appears as a broadcast van, against every camp in the same way. The joke is about the rolling-news format (debates and pundits around the clock); it attributes no stance, statement or fact to the channel or its staff.
- **Current events.** The night "The Street Against the Cost of Living" refers to the day of protests of 17 October 2026, without targeting any organiser, place or participant. A school blockade and its confiscated phones, the Agricultural Show, union marches, minimum-wage workers, burning bins, motorists, farmers, taxes, proxy votes, police, firefighters, tariffs, marches against the pension reform, use of the 49.3 and the Zucman tax (a proposed law named after the economist who put it forward, who does not appear in the game) appear as generic twists in the game. They refer to no specific event, place, date or person, and the game stages no violence: a figure "resigns", a student "goes back to class", a fire "goes out".
- The game is not affiliated with any party, candidate, elected official, company, institution or public service, and is neither endorsed nor funded by any of them.
- It is not a poll, a political advertisement or a call to vote. The "votes" and the "goal" are entirely fictional game mechanics.
- All four camps get the same treatment and all four are playable; each camp's headliner becomes a boss when you play against it.
- No party, channel or company logo is reproduced.

Any person depicted who feels that something goes beyond the limits of satire, or who would like a photo removed, can [write to the publisher](https://mbonnici.fr/en/contact). The request will be reviewed and answered as soon as possible.
